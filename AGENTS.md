# Rooko project guidance

## Agent operating model

- `AGENTS.md` is the project's persistent agent instruction source. Read it before project work and follow it alongside the user request.
- Do not make a `workflow.md` (or similarly named workflow document) the source of agent behavior. Workflow files may be used only when a user explicitly asks for one or this file links to a task-specific plan; they do not override `AGENTS.md`.
- At the start of work, read the root [`context.md`](context.md) and [`progress.md`](progress.md). Keep both current as work proceeds and before handing work back.
- `context.md` is the concise durable project context: product, architecture, decisions, constraints, and important paths. `progress.md` is the current project status: active work, completed items, verification evidence, blockers, and next actions.
- Update context when a durable decision or constraint changes. Update progress when work starts, reaches a meaningful milestone, is verified, or is handed off. Keep both concise; link to detailed plans or evidence instead of copying them.
- Task-specific plans and ledgers under `docs/` or `.superpowers/` are supplemental execution records. They do not replace the root context or progress files.
- Follow the user's current request when it conflicts with older context, and update the context/progress files to reflect the new decision.

## Product authority

- Read [`docs/PRD.md`](docs/PRD.md) for product goals, positioning, final scope, and priorities.
- Read [`docs/SRS.md`](docs/SRS.md) for numbered functional and non-functional requirements and acceptance evidence.
- Treat [`analysis/`](analysis/) as reverse-engineering evidence about the reference app. Keep observations, hypotheses, and new Rooko requirements clearly separate. Preserve the evidence files.
- If an implementation choice conflicts with the PRD or SRS, follow the product documents and record the resolution in the relevant plan or decision note.

## Product and privacy rules

- Rooko is Android-first. Flutter web exists for token preview and tooling; do not present it as a promised product platform.
- Every shipped feature is free. Do not add paid tiers, entitlements, paywalls, billing, purchases, or advertising.
- Do not copy the reference app's name, art, copy, layout, implementation, or unsupported feature claims.
- Do not persist feed text, messages, images, audio, or video. Avoid logging sensitive content or identifiers. After the updated in-app tracking disclosure and affirmative consent, the Instagram/YouTube estimator may read bounded visible labels transiently and immediately hash them in process memory; never log or save raw labels.
- Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics initialize automatically on Android for developer metrics; do not expose a diagnostics control in Settings. Limit custom Analytics to the parameter-free `rooko_app_open` event; disable Analytics advertising ID collection and remove the `AD_ID` permission. Do not add counts, selected apps, observed time, Accessibility signals/content, feed labels, item identifiers, user IDs, custom Crashlytics keys, or content-bearing Crashlytics logs. Firebase setup failures must not block local app use. The public Privacy Policy is https://sites.google.com/view/rooko/home; Settings and onboarding Privacy Policy actions open it in the browser, while the in-app Accessibility disclosure and onboarding Why access explanation remain local.
- Help & Feedback diagnostics stay local. Show an explicit preview before copying an allowlisted summary to the clipboard; exclude counts, observed-time totals, timestamps, device identifiers, feed content, screenshots, media, Accessibility trees, labels, and item identifiers. Never upload or send a report automatically. Feature Request and Support Email actions open the user's email app with the fixed support recipient and subject; Rooko does not send the message.
- Android monitoring requires a clear in-app disclosure, affirmative user consent, and user-controlled permission setup. Keep monitored package handling limited to supported apps and the approved detector design.
- The only in-scope feeds are YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. Keep app selection, package visibility, detection, and current onboarding limited to those two apps; historical research and QA records do not expand product scope.
- The user-approved eight-question onboarding sequence follows Rook's Okay page. Its exact prompts and options are recorded in PRD section 4. This user-supplied copy is an exception to the general reference-copy rule. Treat the health answers as self-reports only; do not infer diagnosis or causality. Keep answers in a local onboarding draft and clear them on completion. On the screen-time question only, the selected answer changes the small heading illustration to Ready, Strained, Injured, or Queasy in option order; default to Ready before selection. Answers never change live count-based Rook state, counting, or Progress.
- The user approved the wording and motion sequence in root `onboarding.mp4` for the story after question 8. Treat the count, recall, and time displays as illustrations derived from the user's selected answer, not measured activity. The time illustration uses 35 seconds per reel; `1,000+` uses 1,000 as its lower-bound input. Keep this story and its recall answer local to onboarding, clear its draft on completion, and never use it for detector totals or Rook state. This user-directed copy is a narrow exception to the general tone and reference-copy rules.
- The user refined the onboarding particle transitions: a scattered corner gather continues into a stronger Hi, Rook flow; the selected question 8 answer drives a dense, staged count sphere that keeps revolving after the final count. The same sphere moves onto the recall page and keeps revolving; Continue carries it left, then it travels to center and disperses on the result page. Moving particles form the human profile afterward. Preserve reduced-motion and background-pause behavior across these transitions.
- Preserve the five-page story, including the “Over the next 10 years” page. After it, onboarding shows a neutral dotted shield around Ready Rook, then a phone-frame privacy explanation. On a fresh install, installed Instagram and YouTube are selected by default; users may change that selection in Settings. Preserve the separate detailed Accessibility disclosure and affirmative consent. A dismissible Accessibility guide precedes Android Settings. The access-status page offers an optional floating-counter opt-in; its separate overlay guide precedes overlay Settings. Keep the access-status page focused on the two permission controls, without per-app availability rows or a duplicate overlay explanation. Verify both grants on return. The former standalone app-selection and floating-counter opt-in pages are not part of onboarding. The final handoff may open only a selected, installed Instagram or YouTube app, or proceed to Today. The reference captures are visual guidance, not authority for background permission, feed blocking, roasting, or guaranteed count claims.
- After updated tracking consent and Accessibility access, use a validated exact-version profile when available. Otherwise, selected installed Instagram and YouTube versions automatically use the package-scoped estimated counter without a debug toggle. Detect the installed version locally; a version string alone does not establish accuracy, and missing feed/item signals fail closed. Other unsupported apps fail closed. Estimated days remain unverified for Progress. Do not claim accuracy beyond measured validation.
- Keep one combined sequence and total of counter-recorded events. Today may show selected-app attribution counts beneath its combined total; Progress Day may show daily app attribution rows alongside the combined total, measured hourly count buckets, and a local aggregate of approximate observed time while a selected recognized Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts feed is foregrounded. The time metric uses short monotonic intervals checked against the active root, discards gaps over five seconds and on any known interruption, and never infers duration from counts, count timestamps, or item dwell. It does not establish playback or attention. Persist only the daily duration aggregate; legacy duration remains unknown. Collect duration only after the updated in-app disclosure and affirmative consent. Overlay access is not required for the metric. Progress Week uses combined counts without app attribution. Tapping a Week date opens its Day detail, including the empty state for dates without records; app resume refreshes the visible period from local records while retaining the previous display if a refresh fails. Week may show the highest recorded day, saved-day coverage, a count comparison only when both adjacent weeks have seven verified days, and summed observed-feed time only when all seven daily duration values are available. Missing days, duration, and saved zero-count days with unknown or partial coverage remain unknown; show zero only for verified count coverage. The floating counter uses only the combined count. Older records without hourly buckets retain their total and show an hourly-detail placeholder. Daily-limit UI, limit-triggered interruptions, and overrides are deferred; saved legacy values are inert. Keep event categories and coverage metadata internal for QA and release gating; do not claim detector accuracy beyond measured validation.
- Rook's eight-state wiring is active: Ready (0–49), Strained (50–99), Injured (100–149), Queasy (150–199), Vomiting (200+), Recovering (Today-only for up to 600 ms after a validated feed exit), Victorious (verified personal-best Progress day), and Sleeping (Today when consent, monitoring, and all eligible profiles are unavailable). A selected Instagram or YouTube version eligible for estimated counting may make Today use a count pose; it never establishes a verified day or Victorious. Ready through Queasy share `assets/rook/rook.riv`; the other poses use dedicated Rive files. Each state has transparent SVG art and eye-only animation. Today and Progress use Rive when foregrounded and motion is allowed; matching SVGs are used for reduced motion, backgrounding, or Rive failure. The floating counter uses static transparent images for the five count poses. Midnight may refresh only an already-visible counter after current version and feed eligibility are re-resolved; cached recognition must not restore a hidden counter. Preserve the user's selected art and references; expressions describe Rook, never the user or their health.

## Code and design conventions

- Flutter/Dart owns shared screens and presentation. Kotlin owns Android-only services, permission state, detector lifecycle, local persistence, notifications, and the typed platform bridge.
- Dart design tokens in `lib/core/design_system/` are canonical. `web/global.css` mirrors their names and values for previews. Update both surfaces together.
- Google Sans is the global UI font. Configure it through `AppTypography` and `AppTheme`, with the same family and system fallbacks in `web/global.css`; keep its OFL license and trademark notice bundled.
- The UI palette is black and white dominant: Light uses white canvas/cards, Dark uses neutral near-black canvas/cards, and AMOLED uses pure black surfaces. Dark and AMOLED selected controls use near-black `#303030` fills with white labels and icons for contrast; general text colors remain unchanged. Use neutral gray also for supporting text, outlines, dividers, and disabled states. Only Rook artwork and supported-app logos retain color; status text and icons are monochrome.
- For supported target-app logos, use the approved colored assets in `assets/supported-app-icons/` instead of generic HugeIcons or recreated marks. Preserve each asset's color and aspect ratio. Use `AppIcons` and `AppIcon` for action, navigation, and status glyphs; do not substitute those glyphs for supported-app identities.
- Use `AppTypography`, `AppColors`, `AppSpacing`, `AppRadii`, `AppTheme`, `AppIcons`, and `AppIcon` instead of local token values or direct HugeIcons glyphs in screens.
- After onboarding, Today is the default destination. Each page title shares one top navigation row with enlarged, icon-only Progress and Settings controls on the right; the icons retain accessible names and 48 dp touch targets. Neither a Today tab nor a bottom navigation bar is shown. A visible Back control stays at the top left on Progress and Settings, and it and Android Back return to Today. Use Progress for the daily-record feature across UI, bridge, repository, and current Room schema; old History identifiers remain only in compatibility migrations and historical evidence.
- Progress panels, the Day/Week selector, and internal separators have no visible border strokes. The Day graph shows slim bars only for hours with recorded counts and a fixed dotted zero baseline; its time labels align to the plot, and the selected readout names the hour window. Initially select the latest counted hour; a zero Today selects the current hour and an earlier zero day selects noon. Tap, drag, and accessibility adjustment update the readout without a second vertical line. New counts save each acceptance timestamp and UTC offset for seven days, so a selected hour can show exact counted times. Older counts retain only their latest known timestamp when available; never fabricate missing event times. Do not describe nonzero count buckets as active or viewing hours.
- Support light, dark, and AMOLED themes. Keep controls accessible to TalkBack and large text; respect reduced-motion settings.
- Keep product copy concise, minimal, and competitive in a self-directed way. Never reward increased viewing or shame the user.
- Preserve existing product documents and REA evidence unless the user explicitly asks to change them.

## Project map

- `lib/`: Flutter application and shared design system.
- `android/`: Android host and future Kotlin platform implementation.
- `web/`: Flutter browser preview and mirrored CSS token surface.
- `assets/`: licensed app assets and fonts.
- `assets/supported-app-icons/`: approved colored logos for supported target apps.
- `docs/PRD.md`, `docs/SRS.md`: product and system requirements.
- `analysis/`: REA research artifacts.
