# Rooko revenue model proposal

**Status:** Proposal for decision, 2026-11-23. No monetization or blocking requirement is approved by this document. The current [PRD](../PRD.md) and [SRS](../SRS.md) still require every feature to be free and limits/interruption to remain deferred.

**Feature-slate update (2026-11-25):** The exploratory UX flows and build order in [PRD section 4.4](../PRD.md#44-candidate-focus-tools--feature-exploration) supersede the feature slices below. That slate removes feed time and count allowances; its sole candidate usage limit covers the whole selected app. The commercial hypotheses below remain historical proposals, not approved scope.

## The user problem and decision

Some people need Instagram or YouTube for creators, messages, or longer videos while wanting less short-form scrolling. The five ideas in the request are **proposals**, not observed Rooko behavior. SocialLite's [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sociallite.android&hl=en-IN) claims that it preserves useful native-app paths while limiting feeds, and shows in-app purchases. REA also inspected the Play-installed SocialLite 3.0.4 APK: it contains separate native Reels/Shorts recognition and full-app blocking/schedule/usage-limit paths, plus a RevenueCat/Play Billing implementation with `pro` and `parent` entitlements. The code proves those paths are shipped, not that they work reliably, what a new user sees after onboarding, which features are paid, or what price the store returns. The public [Android page](https://sociallite.app/for-android/) and [FAQ](https://sociallite.app/faq/) describe Accessibility intervention and subscription pricing; those are the developer's claims, not Rooko demand or Indian pricing evidence. The app was launched once on an OPPO Android 15 device and left at its initial get-started screen; no account, permission, feed, or purchase was exercised. Evidence IDs and limits are in [SocialLite findings](../../analysis/sociallite/FINDINGS.md). The user authorized a paid-model proposal; it does not silently amend Rooko's current all-free PRD/SRS.

## Feasibility of the five ideas

| Idea | What Rooko can credibly offer | Decision for now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Instagram creator allowlist | REA found no positive APK evidence of a creator-only feed. SocialLite's listing says users can intentionally visit profiles, which does not establish a Reels allowlist. A local creator link list can support intentional entry, but Instagram controls the session after handoff. | Treat creator shortcuts as a lightweight discovery feature. Do not promise or sell a feed containing only those creators. |
| 2. Instagram hashtag feed | REA found no positive APK evidence of hashtag-only scrolling. A saved hashtag can open search when deep links work; resulting Reels and later navigation remain controlled by Instagram. | Offer a saved-search shortcut only if testing confirms the destination and users understand the boundary. It is not a filtered feed or a paid anchor. |
| 3. Remove YouTube Shorts, keep long videos | SocialLite's APK contains recognized Shorts container IDs and a YouTube playback service, but REA did not observe whether the Shorts surface is removed or long-form stays available. [YouTube also offers a Shorts feed limit of zero](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16671528), with a dismiss/ignore path. | A Shorts-only boundary is technically plausible through version-aware native-feed recognition, but still unverified. First guide users to YouTube's own setting; prototype a Rooko pause only if users need a distinct boundary and device tests preserve long videos, channels, search, and direct links. |
| 4. Sleep mode for both apps | SocialLite's APK has a time/day sleep-window calculator, clock and boot hooks, and a block screen. Static code shows the feature path; no real sleep interval was exercised. [Android Digital Wellbeing Focus mode](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420) already schedules whole-app pauses. | Test one combined Rooko routine against system Focus mode. Name it a scheduled pause, keep a temporary break, and avoid promising an unbreakable lock. |
| 5. Reels/Shorts blocker or limiter | SocialLite ships a separate feed-recognition service plus a generic whole-app blocker and per-app minute-limit path. No live feed accuracy was measured. Rooko's estimated counts/time are not a sound exact cap until detector validation passes. | Prototype one reversible feed pause. Keep whole-app blocking clearly labeled as broader, and defer count-based caps until coverage is verified. |

**Technical boundary (not a policy review).** Android [UsageStatsManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageStatsManager) observes package usage; it does not identify Reels versus other Instagram surfaces. An ordinary app cannot use Android's device/profile-owner package-suspension API for a system-level hard lock. SocialLite's APK demonstrates a different implementation shape: Accessibility services identify native feed UI, while another service handles whole-app packages, sleep and minute limits. This is evidence of a shipped code path, not proof of effectiveness or of what Rooko should copy. Meta's [official Instagram API](https://www.postman.com/meta/instagram/documentation/6yqw8pt/instagram-api) supports professional-account workflows, not consumer-feed ordering; YouTube's [search API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/reference/rest/v3/search/list) filters search results. Per the user's direction, this proposal does not make current or future store-policy adoption a gate for product exploration.

## Recommended model

| Commercial approach | Advantage | Main cost or risk | Call |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Remain entirely free | Preserves the approved contract and removes billing friction | Requires a separate operating budget | Valid fallback if the focus intervention fails |
| Free tracker plus one-time Focus Plus | Pays for an optional, mostly local utility without recurring charges | New sales must fund compatibility updates and support | **Recommended first paid experiment** |
| Free tracker plus subscription | Predictable recurring revenue if value endures | Needs demonstrable sustained value, higher retention, and careful renewal communication | Revisit only if ongoing service is actually built and wanted |

**Order of product bets:** SocialLite is now a closer implementation reference, not merely a listing comparison: its APK separates native Reels/Shorts recognition from whole-app blocking and per-app schedule/usage enforcement, then connects subscription state to `pro` and `parent` entitlements. This gives Rooko feature-path hypotheses to validate, not reliability proof. First test a reversible Reels/Shorts boundary while preserving useful app paths (idea 5), then a single scheduled routine (idea 4). Help users find YouTube's zero-Shorts setting (idea 3) before building a duplicate. Test creator/hashtag entry shortcuts (ideas 1 and 2) separately; they are not demonstrated competitor capabilities and do not justify a premium feed-filter promise.

## Feature creation plan

| Slice | User-visible behavior and honest fallback | Evidence to collect before selling it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creator entry | Save a small local set of creator links and launch a chosen profile intentionally. Once Instagram opens, its recommendations remain available. Keep it free as a shortcut. | Repeated use in creator interviews; links work on supported Instagram versions; users do not mistake the shortcut for an allowlisted feed. |
| Hashtag entry | Save hashtag shortcuts and open Instagram search when a deep link works; otherwise show the tag for manual search. Search results and subsequent navigation remain Instagram-controlled. Keep it free. | Link, unavailable-tag, and return-path checks; users understand no later Reel is filtered by tag. |
| YouTube choice | Guide users to YouTube's own zero-Shorts limit. A separate Rooko pause, if built, reacts only to recognized Shorts surfaces and leaves long videos, search, and channels reachable. Make the baseline switch free. | Compare the YouTube setting with the Rooko behavior on supported versions; test Home, Shorts, search, deep links, long-video playback, and no-service fallback. |
| Sleep routine | Select Instagram, YouTube, or both; choose weekdays and an overnight interval; preview next start/end and allow a temporary break. A basic routine remains free; advanced multi-routine scheduling is the paid candidate. | Midnight, timezone/DST, reboot, battery restriction, permission loss, service recovery, and override tests. Confirm the user never confuses a local scheduled pause with an OS-enforced lock. |
| Feed pause or allowance | Provide a reversible pause on recognized Reels/Shorts or a reminder based on approximate observed-feed time. Unknown detection and duration stay unknown. Do not implement an exact count cap from estimated counts. Keep one basic boundary free. | Real-app false-pause and missed-pause rates, long-form/DM non-interference, version update and service recovery, and user-reported usefulness. Agree pass thresholds before beta. |

The simplest paid candidate is a **routine builder** only if it works better for target users than YouTube's and Android's built-in controls: multiple schedules, separate app/feed rules, predictable temporary breaks, and compatibility maintenance. One saved routine and the basic effective boundary stay free so the paid offer has demonstrable incremental value. If the routine builder does not clear that bar, these five ideas do not yet support a credible paid product. SocialLite's build uses a recurring model and has account/parent paths; Rooko's current local-first scope does not yet show comparable ongoing service value, so that price structure should not be copied automatically.

1. **Keep the existing tracker free.** Today, Progress, counting, privacy controls, export/deletion, and basic permission recovery stay available without payment. Avoid ads, data sales, and an onboarding paywall. This is a proposed future exception to the all-free contract, not a change to the current build.
2. **Build and validate one free focus boundary first:** a reversible, clearly labeled intervention when a recognized Instagram Reels feed opens. Add a schedule or YouTube-specific intervention only if research shows value beyond built-in controls. Keep each rule off by default, distinct from counting consent, and editable or stoppable by the user. If device testing or eventual Play review rejects it, offer a guide to Android's own controls instead of charging for a weak substitute. Emergency/system navigation and uninstall remain available.
3. **Recommended commercial test: one-time Focus Plus unlock after the free boundary works.** Candidate value is multiple repeating schedules, separate Instagram/YouTube routines, and configurable pause rules, but only if users value these over built-in alternatives. The functionality is mostly local and static, so a one-time product fits better than recurring billing. SocialLite shows that subscriptions can be supported with explicit `pro`/`parent` entitlements, remote offerings, and recurring account services; use recurring billing only if Rooko later offers similarly ongoing value such as cross-device schedules, family management, or maintained hosted services. Do not charge for a creator-only feed, hashtag-only feed, exact count cap, guaranteed lock, or a guide to free system settings.
4. **Use Play Billing for in-app digital access.** Google's [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738) generally requires it for Play-distributed digital features, with enrolled regional exceptions. The [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533) requires sustained or recurring value, so a subscription is not the starting model. Do not route buyers to an outside checkout by default. RevenueCat remains optional billing infrastructure, not a prerequisite or a business model.

**Price hypothesis, not a market fact:** test ₹299 versus ₹499 as a one-time India unlock only after working-value interviews and the gates below. Keep a visible free route. At 10,000 active users, 2% first-time conversion at ₹399 would be ₹79,800 **one-time gross**, before Play fees, taxes, refunds, support, and compatibility maintenance; recurring monthly revenue would be zero without new buyers. At 0.5% conversion the same cohort would gross ₹19,950. This makes retention, new-user acquisition, and maintenance cost as important as conversion. Do not extrapolate Indian prices or revenue from SocialLite's US reference subscription or install counts.

## Validation sequence and gates

| Stage | Work | Proceed only if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Interview 15–20 target users, including creators, about the need to keep useful parts of each app; observe them using YouTube's Shorts limit and Android Focus mode before showing concepts for shortcuts, scheduled pause, and feed pause. | Users can distinguish a shortcut, reminder, interruption, and true OS lock; willingness to pay is tied to a working routine that existing controls do not provide. |
| Feasibility | Prototype on supported Android/Instagram/YouTube versions with an opt-in rule. Record only content-free structural outcomes; test denied/revoked access, app updates, backgrounding, reboot, timezone changes, and overrides. | Independent device evidence shows reliable detection and no unintended interruption of messages, long videos, system screens, or permission recovery. If not, remove the behavior and its claim. |
| Product trust | Put the paywall after a demonstrated use of the free tracker and boundary, triggered when a user asks for a second routine or advanced rule. Explain exactly what remains free and what the one-time purchase adds. | Users understand the price, paid increment, and no-account local core; onboarding completion and boundary use do not depend on payment. |
| Free beta | Release the basic boundary without billing, collect opt-in feedback and content-free reliability metrics. | The boundary demonstrably helps users without raising permission revocation, false interruption, crash, or support rates beyond agreed thresholds. Set thresholds before the beta. |
| Paid test | Run a small Play Billing one-time price test for additional routines. Compare price points with local interviews rather than converting SocialLite's public US FAQ figures or guessing from its product IDs. Track free-to-paid conversion, 30/90-day active use, refunds, support and compatibility maintenance cost, and contribution margin per cohort. | Buyers use the routines and cohort revenue covers realistic compatibility/support cost; otherwise keep the tool free or redesign the offer. |

No participant-level Accessibility content, selected creators/hashtags, feed labels, individual viewing records, or target-app identifiers should be added to Firebase. Local shortcut or rule settings would need a separate retention/deletion design and an updated disclosure where their purpose changes. No API, VPN, UI scraping, synthetic tap, ad-hiding, or cross-app content collection should be assumed acceptable merely because a competitor lists it.

## Contract impact and open decisions

- **PRD:** sections 2–3 (positioning, success measures, all-free scope and sequence), UX-03 (consent), UX-07/08 (limits and interruption), Settings, and release gates would need a coordinated revision **only if** the user approves a monetized focus direction.
- **SRS:** FR-010–021 (recognition/coverage), FR-040–047 (currently deferred), FR-070 (billing-free), FR-072 (analytics limits), NFR-08/10 (offline and policy), data specification, and release gates would need new acceptance criteria. Existing estimated events and incomplete days cannot be treated as exact limits.
- **Open decisions:** Does Rooko stay entirely free? If monetized, is the initial customer a creator needing intentional entry or a general user needing scheduled boundaries? Is a one-time Focus Plus unlock acceptable when the basic effective boundary remains free? Which launch market and Android/device versions are in scope?

Until those decisions and gates pass, keep the current free, non-blocking release plan. Do not add billing dependencies, paywalls, blocker copy, or a promise of creator/hashtag-only feeds to the current app or store listing.
